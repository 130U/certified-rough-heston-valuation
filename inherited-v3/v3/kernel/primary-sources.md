# Primary sources and attribution boundary

- Natalia Kopteva, “Pointwise-in-time a posteriori error control for time-fractional parabolic equations”, [arXiv:2105.05848v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05848v2): equation (2.1) and Remark 2.1 identify the positive inverse kernel, including the zero-damping fractional integral. Theorem 2.2 and Lemma 2.8 use positive-time regularity. The authoritative V2 AC bridge is retained in Appendix C; the present extension does not replace that bridge with a bare citation.
- Thomas Simon, “Mittag-Leffler functions and complete monotonicity”, [arXiv:1312.4513v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.4513v2), published in Integral Transforms and Special Functions 26(1), 36–50 (2015), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10652469.2014.965704): the classical completely monotone Mittag-Leffler results justify positivity of the resolvent for nonnegative damping. This source is distinct from Simon’s arXiv:1310.1888, which concerns other comparisons and useful Mittag-Leffler bounds.
- NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, [Gamma-function asymptotics](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11): supports the identified mature strict Gamma primitive’s analytic remainder framework. The new cumulative kernel proof uses the elementary log convexity consequence Gamma(x) >= 1 for x >= 2; its complete argument is written in the proof rather than inferred from numerical Gamma values.

Borrowed tools are positive fractional resolvents, Caputo comparison/convexity under the established regularity bridge, beta convolution, and validated special-function arithmetic. The local contribution tested here is their explicit composition with the rough-Heston exponent curve, a cumulative pricing-weight series with absolute finite tails, and a complete matched actual-output certificate. The eight negative controls check concrete implementation faults; they do not prove novelty or replace the mathematical argument.
